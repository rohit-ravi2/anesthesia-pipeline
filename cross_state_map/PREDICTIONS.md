# PRE-COMMITTED PREDICTIONS

**Frozen 2026-09-16T12:45:24Z, before any scoring.**

## Status of this file

These predictions **do not gate the table and the table is not built to test them.** The table is
descriptive: it records which mechanisms each state acts on. These are written down only so that the
completed table retains the ability to surprise, and so that outcomes which are merely expected
cannot later be presented as findings.

Author: the scorer, before any scoring; these are not Rohit's predictions. All are made under the contamination
disclosure in `INCLUSION_CRITERION.md` §6.

---

## P1 — REM dissociates the axes

`S3_rem_hsap` and `S3_rem_mmus` will score `A_beh: decreased` with `A_neu` and `A_met` **not**
decreased relative to wake.

*If false* — if REM turns out to be reduced on all three axes — the axis separation is doing less
work than the design assumes, and the strongest argument for keeping three axes weakens.

## P2 — the deep states are untested where it matters most

For `S7_dauer_cele`, `S8_crypto_tard`, `S8_crypto_cele`, `S9_hib_seasonal_isp`: classes 1–5
(membrane conductance and synaptic release) will be predominantly `NOT_TESTED` rather than
`TESTED_NEGATIVE`.

**If true this is the single most valuable output of the table**, because the widely repeated claim
that dormancy reduces neural excitability would then be largely unexamined at the mechanism level in
exactly the states where it is asserted most confidently. That converts a literature commonplace
into a named list of missing experiments.

## P3 — one endpoint, two suites

`S5_ga_volatile_hsap` and `S5_ga_propofol_hsap` will differ in their positive class sets despite a
behavioural endpoint that is clinically indistinguishable.

*If false* — if the two converge on the same suite — that is a substantive result in the other
direction and strengthens any later convergence argument considerably.

## P4 — positives will track study volume (expected, not a finding)

`n_positive` per row will correlate positively with the study volume of that (state, species).
Committed here in advance **so that it cannot be reported as a discovery.** Its only legitimate use
is as the reason the coverage denominator is mandatory.

## P5 — coverage will be grossly uneven

The coverage denominator will vary by at least threefold across the 19 rows, with human sleep and
human anaesthesia highest and tardigrade cryptobiosis lowest.

## P6 — chains will cross classes more often than not

More than half of all scored mechanisms will have `proximal_class` different from at least one
`upstream_class`. If so, the chain-recording rule (`REPERTOIRE.md` §3.2) is load-bearing rather than
bureaucratic, and any prior cross-state comparison that flattened chains to a single level was
comparing incommensurable things.

## P7 — the expression screen will flag something

For the four *C. elegans* rows, the CeNGEN expression-feasibility screen will flag **at least one**
published mechanism claim whose implicated gene is not detectably expressed in the implicated
neurons at the pre-declared threshold.

*This is the riskiest prediction here and the only one whose test uses data rather than literature.*
A null — every literature claim survives the expression check — is a clean result and is reportable.

## What would count as surprising

Recorded now, so that surprise is recognised rather than rationalised:

- any state scoring `TESTED_NEGATIVE` (not `NOT_TESTED`) across all of classes 1–4;
- a cryptobiosis row with grade E-A or E-B evidence at any of classes 1–5;
- a sleep row with no evidence at class 6 or 7;
- two rows of the same committed state name in different species with disjoint positive class sets.
